Thank you for reviewing Dead Set.

WHAT THE APP IS AND WHO IT IS FOR
A setlist-building and listening companion for Grateful Dead fans. The band played
roughly 2,300 concerts between 1965 and 1995 and let their audiences record
and freely trade them, so tens of thousands of concert recordings sit in the
public Internet Archive. It is organised by date,
which makes finding a particular performance of a particular song hard and
comparing performances across years nearly impossible.
Dead Set lets a fan assemble a "dream setlist" -- choosing a specific night's
performance of each song, arranged as a real concert with Set I, Set II and an
Encore -- then play it back and share it. The audience is adult music fans.
The app is free, carries no ads, no purchases, and no monetisation.

SETUP AND ACCESS
No setup required. Browsing and listening need no account; an account unlocks
building, favourites and comments. The demo credentials in the
sign-in fields are an email/password account needing no Google or Apple ID.
Home shows community setlists: tap one, press play to stream. Browse or
"Build with Cosmic Charlie" starts a setlist; the version browser picks the
exact night per song. Profile > Danger Zone is account deletion.

AUDIO CONTENT AND RIGHTS (guideline 5.2)
Dead Set is an independent, non-commercial fan project, not affiliated with,
endorsed by or sponsored by the Grateful Dead, Grateful Dead Productions,
Rhino Entertainment or Warner Music Group. All trademarks belong to their
owners. Stated in the app footer and at https://dead-set.org/about.

The Grateful Dead explicitly permitted audience taping and free
non-commercial trading of their live performances throughout their career,
which is why the Internet Archive's Live Music Archive hosts these recordings
publicly. We honour the non-commercial terms: we host no audio, we stream
from the Archive's public endpoints, we carry no advertising, we sell
nothing. The Archive offers audience tapes as downloads but, at the band's
request, makes soundboards stream-only. Dead Set offers no download anywhere,
and on items marked access-restricted or stream-only it plays the Archive's
own streaming derivative and never requests the restricted original; if no
streamable derivative exists it skips the item. The band's posted policy is
reproduced verbatim in-app at https://dead-set.org/about. Same model as Relisten (App Store ID
715886886), on the store since 2014. We are not in a regulated industry.

EXTERNAL SERVICES
Internet Archive (archive.org), the source of all audio. Relisten
(api.relisten.net), a public concert database used for song order. Supabase
via Lovable Cloud for accounts, database and storage. Lovable AI Gateway, the
language model behind the in-app setlist assistant, which receives a user's
typed request and setlist contents. PostHog for first-party product analytics
-- no advertising and no cross-app tracking, so App Tracking Transparency
does not apply and no tracking permission is requested. Resend for
transactional and opt-in email. No payment processors, as nothing is sold.

REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
None. The app behaves identically in every region: no geographic gating, no
region-specific content, free everywhere.

USER-GENERATED CONTENT (guideline 1.2)
Users publish setlists and comments. The app includes a report flow -- the
flag on any setlist, comment or direct message; user blocking via the icon
beside another person's comment; a moderation queue reviewed by the team; and
in-app account deletion at Profile > Danger Zone. Terms and Privacy Policy
are linked at signup and in-app.

ACCOUNT (guideline 4.8)
Sign in with Apple, Google, or email and password. Sign in with Apple sits
alongside Google on the same screen.

BACKGROUND AUDIO
The app declares the audio background mode and plays with lock-screen
transport via the system player: the core use case is listening to full
concert sets while the phone is pocketed or locked.
