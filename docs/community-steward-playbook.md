# Community Steward — Playbook & Roadmap
*Dead Set · dead-set.org · internal — for the human Community Steward*

Hey Now. This is your operating manual. You own the relationships; the drafting desk (our Claude workspace, via the /steward command) prepares your materials, tracks your rhythm, and hands you a priority list every week. It drafts — you connect. Nothing reaches the community that didn't pass through a person.

## The role in one line

Be the person the Founding Deadheads know is in the room. The two most important things we learned in beta: the community is finding each other, and shared setlists travel friend-to-friend. Your job is to tend both.

## Who you're stewarding

~40 Founding Deadheads (inner circle: dead-set.org/backstage), and the personas behind them:

- **Maya** — new fan, our primary growth driver. Needs a warm welcome and an easy first setlist. Never let a Maya post go unanswered for 24h.
- **Dave** — veteran. Respect his ears; ask his opinion, don't market to him.
- **Russ** — archivist/tape trader. Precision and source credit matter more than enthusiasm.
- **Jess & Marcus** — the social layer. Give them things worth sharing.

## Voice — non-negotiables

"Hey Now" as greeting. "Wake. Now. Discover." (W-A-K-E). Community-centric, never founder-centric — "we are finding each other." Stewardship framing (tapers, traders, the Internet Archive) in every major communication; Archive credit proud and front-of-page. Cosmic Charlie is a character — the encounter itself given a name. The words "AI," "algorithm," and "generate" never appear in anything community-facing, spoken or written. Emails follow Set I / Set II / Encore. Lead with emotional truth, not mechanism.

## Weekly rhythm

**Monday** — pull your steward packet (Jay runs /steward, or you do once onboarded): priority list, pending drafts, content briefs, lexicon proposals. Review, edit in your own hand, schedule.
**Midweek** — welcomes and replies. New members greeted personally within a day. Flag anything odd (bugs, Archive concerns, unhappy members) to Jay same-day.
**Friday** — Friday Set goes out; note what the community reacted to this week and feed it back: exact phrases people use to describe versions (jamminess, intensity, transitions, length) go into the lexicon proposal pile — this vocabulary directly shapes the product.
**Ongoing** — watch for canon signals (era votes, consensus picks like Veneta '72) and surface them; power users drive the canon.

## First 30 / 60 / 90

**Days 1–30 — Listen.** Meet every Founding Deadhead by name. Read the backstage archive. Learn the voice by editing drafts rather than writing cold. Deliver: a "state of the community" note to Jay — who's active, who's drifted, what people love, what they're asking for.
**Days 31–60 — Tend.** Own the weekly rhythm end-to-end. Start one recurring community thread that isn't about the product (show memories, tape stories). Deliver: first month of lexicon feedback shipped; welcome flow for new members documented.
**Days 61–90 — Grow.** Propose the next-cohort plan: how we invite beyond the founding 40 without losing the room's feel. Weigh in on the Mighty Networks question with evidence from your first 60 days. Deliver: written recommendation.

## How you're measured

Not follower count. Ever. What matters: response time to new members, active-member ratio week over week, community threads that run without prompting, saves / bio-link taps / DM conversations on Instagram content, and lexicon contributions that make it into the product. Quality of the room over size of the room.

## Boundaries

You send community communications after Jay's sign-off (first 30 days) and independently thereafter within this playbook. Anything touching legal, the Internet Archive relationship, refunds/money, or press goes to Jay, always. When in doubt: warmth first, accuracy second, speed third.
