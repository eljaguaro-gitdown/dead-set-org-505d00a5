## Hold before sending

1. **Numbers are provisional.** "52 of 52 in our testing" is the only outcome figure used. The "7 of 52 before" comparison is left out on purpose: it was measured on a slower network, so it is not a same-run before/after. Jay said final same-run numbers arrive later today. Hold the dispatch until Jay confirms the figures still stand.
2. **Jay's phrasing I softened:**
   - "put an End to ... never load": not claimed. We can't promise nothing will ever fail to load. The copy says "the endless wait is over: you'll hear something, or know why", which is what the 8-second fallback and the up-front "no tape" label actually deliver.
   - "Instant": the tap is answered instantly (ring and bear). Sound takes a few seconds on a phone. The copy says so and never calls playback instant.
   - "Measurable playback improvements": kept as a feeling ("we're digging the results"), with the numbers labelled as testing. No claim about speed on real phones in the field, since that data starts with this release.
3. **"Tape index" is not fan copy.** It reads as machinery. Rendered as "a shelf list of which tape carries which night".
4. **iPhone figures.** Set I no longer says "most taps were blocked": that was our iPhone emulation, which is stricter than a real iPhone (real fans saw 7 blocked plays in 30 days). Set II keeps "zero blocked taps in our testing", which is what the emulation showed after the fix. "52 of 52" is labelled as phone-sized browser tests, because the WebKit iPhone profile played 17 of 52 in the same run, mostly the test browser's own slowness.
5. **Reporters are credited as a community, never by name.** The credit-on-Build-Notes list (community_fixes) is a separate mechanism and is not touched here.
6. Words checked against the banned list (AI, algorithm, generate, engine, pipeline, model) and internal names. None appear. No emoji in any subject. One 😊 per body.

## 2. Dispatch email

**Subject:** Every tap gets an answer now
**Preview line:** The wait, the silence, the iPhone refusals: sorted, thanks to your reports and the Internet Archive's tapes.

Hey Now,

Every tape on Dead Set lives at the Internet Archive, rolled by tapers and kept moving by traders. You told us when they wouldn't spin, and we've been digging into what came back. 😊

**SET I**
You tapped a song and got a spinner, then silence. Sometimes it ran 25 seconds. On iPhones it could be worse: the player started sound a beat too late after your tap, and the phone refused to play it at all. That's a dead vibe, and you told us so.

**SET II**
- **Every tap is answered at once.** A ring and a little dancing bear appear where your finger landed, within milliseconds, where the old player took over a second. Then a "Digging through the crates" card: *Pulling the tape off the Internet Archive. It'll start on its own.*
- **No more waiting on silence.** If a tape is slow, by 8 seconds a sure-thing tape of the same song starts, with an honest card about it.
- **Nights with no tape say so up front.** Tap one anyway and a sure thing of that song plays, not a dead end.
- **iPhones stopped refusing.** Zero blocked taps in our testing.
- **Faster starts.** We keep a shelf list of which tape carries each song on each night, so a tap goes straight to the tape.
- **On the bench:** every night with a tape played in our phone-sized browser tests (52 of 52).

**ENCORE**
Straight talk: sound still takes a few seconds on a phone, and some old tapes are stubborn. But the endless wait is over. You'll hear something, or you'll know why. Thank you to the Internet Archive, to the tapers and traders, and to everyone who sent a "that one won't play." We're finding each other, one tape at a time.

Go spin something: dead-set.org

Gratefully in it with you,
grateful_jaguaro
