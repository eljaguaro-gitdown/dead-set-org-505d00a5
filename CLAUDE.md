# Dead Set

A web + iOS app for building, sharing, and browsing Grateful Dead setlists. Originally scaffolded by [Lovable](https://lovable.dev/) (`vite_react_shadcn_ts`), backed by Supabase, wrapped for iOS via Capacitor.

## Product & voice grounding — read before writing ANY user-facing copy

The canonical internal grounding document is [`dead-set-field-guide.md`](dead-set-field-guide.md) ("The Tape Box"). Read it before touching landing copy, the share/viewer surfaces, dispatch email, changelog entries, or anything Cosmic Charlie "says." It covers the mission, Charlie's voice model, the recommendation engine, conversion strategy, beta learnings, and the brand system. **The guide is internal** — it describes the technical machinery freely *because it's for builders*; that language must not leak into user-facing surfaces.

**Non-negotiable rules (from the guide — internalize these):**

- **The word "AI" never appears in any user-facing surface.** Cosmic Charlie is a *fan character* — never a "tool," "algorithm," "model," "AI," or "feature." Deep crates and strong opinions, not a pipeline.
- **Internal machinery stays internal.** Gemini, the function-calling pipeline, Lovable, PostHog, "recommendation engine" — none of that language reaches the room where fans are.
- **Voice is taper culture, not media-player generic.** Describe music structurally/energetically (jamminess, length, intensity, transitions, era, lineup) — never genre adjectives. Recordings "circulate" / are "on tape" (this is why [`/setlist/:id`](src/pages/SetlistPoster.tsx) says "on tape," not "have audio").
- **Community is the hero** — not the app, not the founder. Lead with feeling, not feature: "We are finding each other," never "our matching engine."
- **Fixed signals:** tagline **"Wake. Now. Discover."** (W-A-K-E, never "Wait"); salutation **"Hey Now"**; handle **`grateful_jaguaro`** (lowercase); dispatches structured **Set I / Set II / Encore**; subject lines carry no emojis; the **Internet Archive / tapers / traders credit is prominent, never buried**.
- **Every share sells what it is, and the music is one tap away.** A shared link names the thing it carries — the song, the Songbook issue, the setlist, the night, the app — and closes by inviting a press of play, never with a bare brand stamp. All share copy comes from [`src/lib/shareCopy.ts`](src/lib/shareCopy.ts); a surface that assembles its own string is a bug. The playback control belongs **above the fold**, not at the foot of the page.
- **Stewardship posture:** Dead Set is **additive** to the fan ecosystem (headyversion, Relisten, the Archive itself) — never competitive. Point back to them with respect.

A setlist is a *reconstructed night with an arc*, not a song playlist — and every reconstruction carries at least one rare-placement "aha moment." See §2 and §4 of the guide.

## Stack

- **Frontend:** React 18 + TypeScript, Vite 5 (SWC), React Router 6, TanStack Query 5
- **UI:** Tailwind CSS 3 + shadcn/ui (Radix primitives), `lucide-react`, `framer-motion`, `sonner` toasts, `next-themes`
- **Forms / validation:** `react-hook-form` + `zod` (`@hookform/resolvers`)
- **Drag & drop:** `@dnd-kit/*` (setlist reordering)
- **Charts:** `recharts`
- **Auth & data:** Supabase (`@supabase/supabase-js`) + Lovable OAuth wrapper (`@lovable.dev/cloud-auth-js`) for Google/Apple/Microsoft sign-in
- **Mobile:** Capacitor 8 (iOS + Android scaffolds; iOS is the active target)
- **Testing:** Vitest + Testing Library (jsdom) for unit/integration; Playwright (via `lovable-agent-playwright-config`) for E2E
- **Lint:** ESLint 9 (flat config) + `typescript-eslint`, `eslint-plugin-react-hooks`, `eslint-plugin-react-refresh`
- **Package manager:** Bun — **`bun.lock`** (text) is the live lockfile; `package-lock.json` is also maintained. `bun.lockb` is a vestigial binary lockfile from the 2025 scaffold, untouched since, and consulting it will mislead you (see the correction below).

## Data model (Supabase `public` schema)

Source of truth lives in `supabase/migrations/`; mirrored as TS types in [`src/integrations/supabase/types.ts`](src/integrations/supabase/types.ts).

**Core setlist domain**
- `setlists` — owned by `creator_id`, flags for `is_public` / `is_collaborative`, `share_token`, counters (`play_count`, `upvote_count`, `playable_slot_count`), optional `era_id`
- `setlist_slots` — ordered songs in a setlist; `(set_number, position)`, plus optional `notable_version_id`, `segue_to_next`, `notes`
- `setlist_upvotes`, `setlist_comments`, `collaborators` (role enum: owner/editor/viewer)
- `draft_setlists` — anonymous/guest in-progress setlists
- `songs` — Grateful Dead song catalog (title, tags, play stats, `typical_set_position` enum: opener/early/mid/late/closer/encore, `is_jam_vehicle`)
- `notable_versions` — flagged specific performances of a song
- `eras` — band era taxonomy
- `setlist_slot_playability` — precomputed audio availability per slot (see `precompute-slot-playability` function)

**Users / social**
- `profiles` — user-facing profile (handle, etc.); referenced by `useAuth` / `PickHandleModal`
- `user_roles` — RBAC (`app_role` enum: admin/moderator/user), checked via `has_role` RPC
- `favorites`, `favorite_songs`, `favorite_song_setlists`
- `conversations`, `conversation_members`, `direct_messages`, `chat_messages`
- `comment_notifications`

**Engagement / analytics**
- `page_visits`, `visitor_attribution`, `share_events`, `play_events`, `auth_events`
- `ab_test_assignments` (+ `mark_ab_conversion` RPC)
- `admin_traffic_stats_cache` (+ `refresh_admin_traffic_stats`, `get_admin_traffic_stats` RPCs)
- `hero_spotlights`, `announcements`, `announcement_reads`, `changelog_entries`

**Email pipeline**
- `email_sends`, `email_send_log`, `email_send_state`, `email_unsubscribe_tokens`, `suppressed_emails`
- `dispatch_sends` (+ `dispatch_recipients` view)
- RPCs: `enqueue_email`, `read_email_batch`, `move_to_dlq`, `delete_email`

**Cosmic Charlie (AI assistant)**
- `cosmic_charlie_history` (+ `cosmic_charlie_song_frequency` view), driven by the `ai-deadhead` edge function

**Misc:** `insider_bugs`, `insider_shares`, `insider_wishlist`

**Enums:** `app_role`, `changelog_tag`, `collaborator_role`, `set_position`.

Edge functions worth knowing about: `og-image` (renders share-card OG images, called directly from the client by URL), `track-visit` (anonymous visit logging, JWT-free), `join-setlist` (token redemption), `ai-deadhead` (Cosmic Charlie chat), `resolve-song-share` (fuzzy-matches an Archive.org track title back to a `songs` row for the song-share deep-link flow), the email/dispatch family, and the admin-only `admin-users` / `daily-user-report` / `weekly-insights-report`.

## Conventions

- **Import alias:** `@/` → `src/` (configured in `vite.config.ts`, `vitest.config.ts`, `tsconfig`, `components.json`). Always import via `@/components/...`, `@/lib/...`, etc.
- **shadcn/ui:** components live in `src/components/ui/` and are generated by the shadcn CLI (`components.json`: style `default`, base color `slate`, CSS variables on, no prefix). Treat them as project code you can edit, but expect re-generation to overwrite — prefer composition over modification.
- **Design tokens:** colors are HSL CSS variables defined in `src/index.css` and consumed via `hsl(var(--token))` in [`tailwind.config.ts`](tailwind.config.ts). The `dead.*` palette (red, blue, gold, dark, orange, cream, pink, green, purple, rose, surface, surface-hover) is the brand palette — use it instead of raw Tailwind colors for anything themed.
- **Fonts:** the "Deadhead Archives" stack, defined in [`tailwind.config.ts`](tailwind.config.ts) and rationalized in [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) §2 — `font-title` (UnifrakturMaguntia: hero titles, logos, playlist names), `font-header` (Sancreek: era/section dividers), `font-ticket` (Special Elite → Courier Prime: anything representing a physical record, ticket, or archive document), `font-display` (Sancreek, falling back to Playfair Display), `font-hand`/`font-marker` (Caveat), `font-body` (DM Sans), `font-mono` (IBM Plex Mono: functional UI — search, lists, nav). **`DESIGN.md` governs the visual system and explicitly supersedes the older brand guidance in `dead-set-field-guide.md`** — when a UI decision and DESIGN.md disagree, DESIGN.md wins. The field guide still governs *voice and copy*.
- **Routing:** `react-router-dom` with `BrowserRouter`. The landing page (`Index`) is eager-loaded; every other route is `lazy()` in [`src/App.tsx`](src/App.tsx) inside a `<Suspense fallback={null}>`. New routes must go ABOVE the `*` catch-all.
- **Data fetching:** TanStack Query (single `QueryClient` in `App.tsx`). The Supabase client in [`src/integrations/supabase/client.ts`](src/integrations/supabase/client.ts) is a singleton with `persistSession` + `autoRefreshToken` on localStorage — never instantiate a second client.
- **Auto-generated files — do not hand-edit:**
  - [`src/integrations/supabase/types.ts`](src/integrations/supabase/types.ts) (regenerate via Supabase CLI when schema changes)
  - [`src/integrations/lovable/index.ts`](src/integrations/lovable/index.ts) (Lovable OAuth wrapper)
- **Supabase migrations:** append-only, timestamped `YYYYMMDDHHMMSS_<uuid>.sql`. Never rewrite a merged migration; add a new one. After applying schema changes, regenerate `src/integrations/supabase/types.ts`.
- **Edge functions:** per-function `verify_jwt` is configured in [`supabase/config.toml`](supabase/config.toml). Public endpoints (`track-visit`, `og-image`, unsubscribe handlers, `ai-deadhead`, `admin-users`) set `verify_jwt = false` and must validate auth themselves. Authenticated endpoints (`process-email-queue`, `send-transactional-email`, `send-beta-nudge`, `send-dispatch`) rely on Supabase to enforce JWT.
- **Anonymous users:** the app supports anonymous setlist drafts — see [`src/lib/anonSession.ts`](src/lib/anonSession.ts) and the `draft_setlists` table. Don't assume `auth.uid()` is non-null on the client.
- **ESLint:** `@typescript-eslint/no-unused-vars` is intentionally OFF; `react-refresh/only-export-components` is a warning. Don't reintroduce unused-vars as an error without a reason.
- **Test files:** colocated with source as `*.test.ts(x)` / `*.spec.ts(x)` under `src/`, or grouped in a `__tests__/` subdirectory next to the code under test (see `src/lib/charlie/__tests__/`). Either pattern is accepted — the Vitest glob `src/**/*.{test,spec}.{ts,tsx}` picks both up. The Vitest config does not pick up tests outside `src/`.
- **TypeScript:** `strict` mode per the standard Vite React template; project references split into `tsconfig.app.json` (app) and `tsconfig.node.json` (build tooling).
- **PWA:** service worker at [`public/sw.js`](public/sw.js), manifest at [`public/manifest.json`](public/manifest.json), install prompt handled by [`src/components/PwaInstallBanner.tsx`](src/components/PwaInstallBanner.tsx).

### Corrections (banked from gate findings)

- **Verify commits by sha, never by branch name.** When a release context cites a PR/commit/branch, run `git cat-file -t <sha>` against this repo before trusting it — identical branch names exist on both the orphaned `dead-set-org` repo and this repo (`dead-set-org-505d00a5`) with entirely different commit histories, and a PR was once opened against the wrong remote because of it.
- **qa-release must confirm tool access at session start.** A gate session needs Lovable MCP access (`get_diff`/`list_edits`) for the sync check and live-URL reachability (browser through the session's network policy) for the browser items. If either is unavailable, those items are NEEDS-VERIFICATION by construction and the overall verdict cannot be PASS — run the gate from an environment that has both.
- **Lovable "Publish" does NOT deploy edge functions from GitHub-synced commits.** deploy_project ships the frontend only; functions under supabase/functions/ deploy only when the Lovable agent runs supabase--deploy_edge_functions (ask it via send_message: "deploy all edge functions unchanged — zero code changes"). After any merged change to supabase/functions/, request that deploy and VERIFY it landed by probing the live function (pg_net http_get from the production DB works; check a header or behavior that the change altered). The signup-email fix sat merged-but-undeployed for two days because of this.
- **Brand sweep must cover edge-function error strings.** Grep `supabase/functions/*/index.ts` error strings and their `toast.error(e.message)` surfacing sites, not just .tsx JSX text — Cosmic Charlie edge-function error copy ("AI credits exhausted") reaches users verbatim and slips past a JSX-only scan.
- **Brand sweep must match against extracted string literals, not raw grep output.** Pull the quoted string content out first, then test it for banned words. Matching whole `grep -rn` lines lets paths like `ai-deadhead/index.ts` and `generate-setlist-description/index.ts` false-positive on `ai\b` / `generat` — a wall of filename noise that a real user-facing hit hides inside.
- **Static verification silently conflates "defined" with "wired."** Code that looks instrumented can be dead or depend on infrastructure invisible to a repo-only sweep. Two live examples found by one gate on 2026-08-15: every `window.posthog?.capture(...)` call site was optional-chained while nothing in the repo loaded or `.init()`ed the SDK, so those events were no-ops; and the `draft_setlists` + RLS anonymous-draft architecture has no caller outside its own test, while the shipping mechanism is session-scoped `sessionStorage`. **The PostHog half is fixed — don't re-report it.** Since `a4060b8` (2026-09-18) `src/main.tsx` calls `initializePostHog()` before render, and that function ends with `Object.assign(window, { posthog })` — the npm `posthog-js` does *not* set `window.posthog` itself, so that line is what makes the `window.posthog?.capture` sites work. What remains true: `initializePostHog()` returns early, silently in production, if `VITE_PUBLIC_POSTHOG_PROJECT_TOKEN` or `VITE_PUBLIC_POSTHOG_HOST` is unset, and then every `window.posthog?.` call is a no-op again. Check the env, not the init call. The `draft_setlists` half still holds. When a checklist item names a mechanism, grep for **callers**, not definitions, and report "defined but never called" as its own finding — distinct from "unobservable because no browser."
- **Check BOTH the Actions run history and App Store Connect before claiming anything about builds.** This correction has now been wrong in both directions. First a session saw no run history, concluded the TestFlight pipeline "had never run green," and reported it as a submission risk — twice, in writing — while App Store Connect held **19 completed uploads**, all Ready to Submit. The fix banked here then over-rotated and asserted builds 13–19 "went up from Xcode, which leaves no trace here." Also false: `mcp__github__actions_list` shows **19 successful `workflow_dispatch` runs** of `ios-testflight.yml` between 2026-08-07 and 2026-08-21, each ending in `Upload succeeded`, and run 19's date matches build 19's exactly. The trace was here the whole time; nobody listed the runs. Neither source alone is authoritative: ASC knows what exists, the run history knows what put it there. To map one to the other, compare timestamps — an ASC build's creation time is one workflow-duration (3–4 min) after its run's start time, which pins run N to build N without guessing.
- **Derive the CI build number from the run history, not from a story about it.** `ios-testflight.yml` sets `CURRENT_PROJECT_VERSION` from `github.run_number + BUILD_NUMBER_OFFSET`. An offset of **20** was committed on the reasoning that builds 1–19 predated the workflow — but the workflow *made* builds 1–19, at run_number with no offset, so the next run (run 20) would have uploaded build **40**. The offset is now **1**: run 20 + 1 = build 21. The near-miss is the lesson, not the arithmetic — the offset was set to defend against a collision that could not happen, and an ASC build number is permanent once burned. Before touching the offset, list the workflow runs and read the last one's build number out of its own log.
- **`bun install` only works where the private registry is reachable.** `bun.lock` resolves through `europe-west4-npm.pkg.dev` (Lovable's cache). CI reaches it; an external sandbox gets 403 on every package. So **adding an npm dependency has a real cost** — the lockfile cannot be regenerated outside that environment, and a patch that changes `package.json` without `bun.lock` builds locally and silently ships without the dependency in CI. This is why `DeepLinkPlugin.swift` is hand-written instead of using `@capacitor/app`: ~40 lines of Swift beat a dependency that could not be locked.
- **Supabase auth config is in Lovable Cloud, not supabase.com.** This project uses Lovable Cloud, so its Supabase project is provisioned and owned by Lovable — it does **not** appear in the account owner's own Supabase org, and a direct dashboard link 403s. Redirect URLs, Site URL, providers and the anonymous-users toggle are at **Lovable → project → Cloud → Users → Auth settings**. Lovable's own docs say auth settings "live in your Supabase project, not in Lovable", which is true for a *connected* project and wrong for Cloud.
- **An RLS policy is not the whole access story — check column grants too.** `profiles` carries `SELECT USING (true)` for `public`, which reads like every column is world-readable. It is not: column-level grants to `anon`/`authenticated` cover only `avatar_url, created_at, display_name, home_state, id, user_id`, and `dispatch_unsubscribe_token` and the preference flags are already revoked. Reading the policy alone produced a false "exposed unsubscribe token" alarm and a proposed migration that would have changed nothing. Query `information_schema.column_privileges` before concluding a column is exposed.
- **Count every character-limited App Store field; never trust the heading.** `metadata.md` labelled its promotional text "170 chars" and the string was 175 — Apple would have rejected the paste. The verified counts now live next to each field in that file. Same class of error as the screenshot spec, which said 6.7"/6.9" while the actual listing asks for 6.5" only: when a document describes an external system's constraint, check the system.
- **When a commit deletes a feature, sweep beyond `src/` for dangling references.** Check `scripts/`, `public/` (including `sw.js` precache lists), `index.html`, `manifest.json`, and any other non-bundled tooling. A `src/`-scoped sweep looks clean while dev scripts and precached asset paths still point at deleted files — that's how `scripts/generate-signoff.mjs` survived the DJ intro removal and had to be cleaned up in a follow-up commit.
- **`npm ci` failing is its own checklist finding, not a build-tool inconvenience.** When it fails on lockfile drift, do not quietly fall back to `npm install` and mark the build item green — CI runs `npm ci` and will fail exactly where the gate would have caught it. Report the failure verbatim with the commit that introduced the drift. Found when `@lovable.dev/vite-plugin-dev-server-bridge` and `-hmr-gate` sat in `package.json` from `6b8dbd1` but never reached `package-lock.json`; `npm install` worked, which is what hid it.
- **Run the gate against the commit being shipped, not whatever the worktree has checked out.** A gate session reported "2 TypeScript errors, pre-existing, unrelated to this release" while `origin/main` actually had **18**. It was correct about its own working tree — a branch based one commit before the bot commits — and that tree was not what was shipping. `git checkout` the target sha (or `git -c ... --work-tree`) before running `tsc`, `vitest` or a build, and state in the report which sha the numbers came from. A verdict that does not name its commit is not a verdict.
- **Regenerating `types.ts` is a code change and needs a typecheck, not just a commit.** The songbook migration added 7 columns to `notable_versions`; the regenerated Row type then broke **16 call sites across 11 files** that build synthetic versions (an Archive.org recording reconstructed as a `NotableVersion`) from object literals. The bot commit that regenerated it shipped straight to `main` with no PR and no build. - **Never run a mutation loop over an uncommitted working tree.** Both restore mechanisms ate the work in one session. First, two loops — one backgrounded by the 120s timeout, one foreground — shared `/tmp/_bak_src`: the foreground restore consumed the backup, then the background loop's final `rm -rf src; cp -r $BAK src` ran with nothing to copy and **deleted `src/` outright**. Save the re-apply as a script in the scratchpad while iterating, so a lost tree costs seconds. And do not background a loop that mutates the tree at all — the 120s timeout turning a foreground loop into a concurrent one is what started this.

- **A source-scanning guard is a claim about spellings nobody has typed yet, and it loses. Count the thing instead.** The "no url appended to a payload's text" sweep was written to pin the defect that BLOCKED a release, and across three gate rounds it was defeated nine ways. Round one: it matched template literals only, so a concatenated `t + " on Dead-Set.Org"`, a lowercase `on dead-set.org` and an escaped `\u{1F339}` walked past. Keep the sweep as a cheap first net if you like, but never let it be the only thing standing behind a defect that already shipped once. The same applies to any "nobody writes X" guard: it is testing your imagination, not the code.

- **A function that returns `null` for three different reasons has thrown away the thing its caller most needs — and a comment saying so does not bring it back.** `resolveSlot` returned `null` for "the song is not on this tape" (an answer about the music), "the Archive did not respond" (an admission about the network), and "the generic search came back empty". `playSingle` reported all three as **"That song isn't on this tape."**, so a reader got told a tape was missing seconds before that same tape started playing — reported from a phone as "the song not found, but they ARE there". - **Between the tap and the sound, the screen has to say something.** Resolving a night means asking the Internet Archive for the recording and then for the track inside it: a few hundred milliseconds on good signal, seconds on bad, with each fetch capped at 12s and a retry behind it. - **A mock that flattens the distinction the code branches on makes its regression test inert — and the test still passes, which is how it gets committed.** `resolveSlot` calls `findTrackInRecordingDetailed`, which returns `{ url, unreachable }`, and the whole point of the early-return regression was what happens when `unreachable` is TRUE. When a resolver's return type carries a discriminator, the mock must carry it too: mock the function the code actually calls, not a sibling, and make the discriminator a parameter of the test rather than a constant. Same family as the `mockRejectedValue` lesson (a failing status is a resolved promise) — both are a test feeding an input production never produces.

- **When a guard is a conjunction, a test can only reach the half the other half does not already block.** The success-path setState guard is `seq === playSingleSeqRef.current && prev.playingSlot?.id === slot.id`. Four tests, then five, all passed with the `seq` half DELETED, because every one of them used two slots with different ids and the id half refused the stale run on its own. So a passing test proved nothing about the clause it was written for. Before trusting a test of a compound guard, delete each conjunct separately and watch it go red for each; if one survives, the test is reaching the other. - **Commit before mutating, and keep the mutation loop in a script.** Banked once already after two destroyed working trees; it paid for itself three times this round. - **A comment that bounds a cost against a timeout must be MEASURED with every request hung, not reasoned from one request's cap.** I wrote that the heal adds "one extra search, bounded by its own 12s cap and the 25s watchdog". The first half was right and the second was not: the stored-tape leg is 12s + a 0.6s retry wait + 12s before the heal is even reached, so the verdict lands at ~37s and the watchdog banner has been up since 25.6s. - **A fixture can hide a defect for years, and the test that covers the sentence is the one most likely to contain it.** `Math.floor(days / 365.25)` calls two calendar years "a year" whenever no leap day falls in the span (730 days → 1.998). The test asserting exactly this sentence used 1971-03-24 to 1973-03-24 — which contains Feb 29 1972, so 731 days, which floors to 2 and passes. The window is only a few days wide per span, so the copy would have been wrong on a handful of songs indefinitely. When a function converts a continuous quantity into a word, test the boundary on BOTH sides (the anniversary, and one day short), and pick fixtures that do not quietly sit on the safe side of it. - **Check a verification's exit code, not the exit code of whatever you piped it into.** `npx tsc --noEmit -p tsconfig.app.json 2>&1 | tail -2; echo "tsc=$?"` prints `tsc=0` unconditionally — it is `tail`'s status. - **A race test's title names a mechanism; probe once what the player actually DID at the assertion point.** The "Next, pressed while a tap is still resolving" test was titled as a skip to the next track and passed for a different reason: a bare `playSingle` carries no playlist context, so it emptied `playlistSlots`, and `transport.next()` found nothing to advance to and STOPPED the player. This is the second time this session a title promised a behaviour the body never reached, and the third test that passed for the wrong reason, so the rule is not "write better titles": **assert the end state explicitly** (the player is empty, the toast says End of setlist) rather than `not.toBe(previous)`, because a vague assertion is what lets the mechanism drift out from under the name. - **A push can succeed and ship nothing: check `git branch --show-current` before trusting it.** A gate run left the SHARED checkout in detached HEAD (it ran `git checkout <sha>` there instead of in a worktree). Two commits were then made on that detached HEAD, and `git push -u origin main` pushed the `main` *branch* — still sitting three commits back — printed "branch 'main' set up to track 'origin/main'", and exited 0. Nothing was rejected because nothing was attempted. The commits existed, the tree was clean, `git log` looked right, and `origin/main` never moved. The misreading that followed cost more than the mistake: Lovable's `latest_commit_sha` showed the older sha, which was correctly reported as "not synced yet" — and the real cause was that the commit had never left the machine. **Before any push that matters, confirm the branch; after it, confirm `git rev-parse origin/main` is the sha you meant.** The recovery is `git merge-base --is-ancestor main HEAD` then `git branch -f main HEAD` — no history rewritten. And dispatch every gate with an explicit instruction to work in its own worktree and leave the shared checkout on its branch, because the damage is invisible until a push silently no-ops.

- **A one-time backfill in a repo is a statement about every future apply, not just the one you ran by hand.** The Build Notes migration was applied through `query_database` and the file committed afterwards. Replayed, it would not have been idempotent — it would have been destructive: `set published = true where published = false` publishes every draft sitting in the queue at that moment, which is the exact opposite of what a draft is; `set edition_title = … where shipped_on >= '2026-10-05'` is open-ended *forward*, so it renames every edition shipped from then on; and updates keyed on entry titles re-date any future row that reuses one. None of that is visible in a diff, because each statement is correct for the data in front of it. **Bound every statement in a backfill to the rows it is for** — `created_at < <the migration's own timestamp>` is usually enough — so a second apply is inert rather than merely idempotent-looking. Found by a gate asking the one question nobody had: what happens if this runs again?

- **Order test fixtures the way the QUERY returns rows, not the way they are convenient to type.** Deleting the min-tracking line from the Build Notes grouper left all 13 tests green. Every fixture listed its earliest date first, so the group's first row was its minimum and the running max alone produced the right label. Production queries `order("shipped_on", { ascending: false })`, so the first row is the MAXIMUM, and with min tracking gone every edition heading would have read "max–max" — the label-disagreeing-with-its-entries defect that the whole module exists to prevent, reintroduced by the direction the fixtures were typed in. **Write at least one fixture in the real query's order**, and when grouping code tracks a running min and max, mutate each separately: one of them is usually load-bearing only in the order you did not test.

- **When a schema change makes a column nullable, grep every query that FILTERS or KEYS on it.** `.eq(col, null)` matches nothing in PostgREST — it does not error, it returns zero rows, and the surrounding code reports success. Making `week_number` nullable left the Build Notes admin publishing with `.eq("week_number", week.week_number)`, so Publish on a new draft would have toasted "is live" and published nothing, and Discard would have deleted nothing, with the draft still sitting in the queue looking untouched. That is the same failure that left an entire edition unpublished and the page looking frozen. The check costs one grep and belongs BEFORE the migration, not after it: a tolerant reader is not a tolerant writer.

- **A `created_at` bound makes a backfill replay-safe for NEW rows and leaves old ones wide open.** The gate did not read the migration, it ran it: a throwaway Postgres with the real DDL, seeded with rows on both sides of the bound. New rows survived a replay byte-identical, including ones created in the same minute as the bound. Then it seeded *hand edits to pre-bound rows* and replayed again — and the backfill re-published an entry that had been deliberately unpublished, reverted a corrected `shipped_on`, and overwrote an edition title someone had changed by hand. All three are inherent to a bounded backfill and invisible in the diff, because each statement is still correct for the data it was written against. So "idempotent" and "no-op on replay" are different claims: the second one is only true while nobody has touched the rows since. A marker table or a guard on the apply date is the full fix; short of that, verify before trusting a replay. Verified in production here — 0 unlisted rows, 0 pre-bound drafts, 0 rows created between the last real row and the bound — so a replay today is a true no-op, but that is a fact about the data, not about the file.

- **Mutation tests that only ever check a rule is ENFORCED will not notice when it stops applying where it should.** Three tests pinned that a stored `week_label` cannot relabel a derived week, and the mutant that deleted `key === ARCHIVE_KEY ||` — leaving the override refused *everywhere*, archive included — survived all nineteen. Every test asserted the refusal; none asserted the one place the override must still be honoured. The archive is the single edition that cannot describe itself (most of what it covers predates the repo's history, and the four entries that survived into it are all dated August), so dropping its override prints "Aug 13–21, 2026" over an edition spanning Apr 21 to Aug 24 — a heading narrower than its own contents, the same class of lie as the five-month "week" the whole module replaced. **When a guard has an exemption, test the exemption as hard as the rule**: a mutant that widens a restriction is as real as one that removes it.

- **`vitest` exits 1 on an UNHANDLED REJECTION even when every test passes, and "Tests 749 passed" is printed directly above it.** CI was red on ten consecutive pushes to `main` while every one of those commit messages said the suite was green. Both statements were true. `cueingLifecycle.test.tsx` mocks the Supabase client without `rpc`, and `playSetlist` calls `supabase.rpc("increment_play_count")` whenever it is given a setlistId — which throws *outside* any assertion, so no test fails and the run still exits 1 with `Errors 2 errors` on its own line. Three things hid it. The grep used to read the result (`grep -E "Tests +[0-9]+"`) matched only the Tests line and filtered the Errors line out of view. The exit code was never checked, because locally it is 1 anyway: `src/test/anonDraftRls.test.ts` needs Supabase egress this sandbox lacks, so a non-zero exit was the expected background state and a second, real cause of it was invisible underneath. And the pre-release gate reported the two unhandled errors in its notes — correctly, in writing — and it read as a restatement of the egress failures rather than a separate finding. **Run `npx vitest run --exclude 'src/test/anonDraftRls.test.ts'` and check `$?` on its own line: that is the CI-equivalent signal, and it is 0 only when CI will be green.** Grep for `Errors` as well as `Tests`. And when a known-failing test makes the exit code permanently non-zero, the exit code has stopped being a signal — find a way to subtract it rather than learning to ignore it, because everything else that breaks hides in the same place. (The three older AudioPlayerContext suites already mocked `rpc`, which is why CI was green until the commit that added the fourth.)

- **When a release moves a link away from a page that carries a safety control, re-run Apple's 1.2 list from the new destination.** The Songbook shelf's community card used to open `/setlist/:id`, which has "Report or block". A release pointed it at `/songbook/:slug` so the guide could be shared as an issue. That page printed the contributor's name and their note on every night, with no report, flag or block control and no link back to the setlist. The comment above the link even said "the guide is one click on from there", and it was not. Every safety control's own tests still passed, because none of the controls had changed; what changed was which page a fan lands on. It shipped in `a096ff3`, was on dead-set.org from the `aea4049` publish (2026-10-06), and was caught only by gate 6 (2026-10-07, on `f9656c5`), a week after build 27 was rejected under the same guideline. It also exposed a second gap: `setlist_slots.notes` had no language filter, because notes had been private until that page began printing them. Two rules. **A link change is a safety change** when the old destination carried report or block and the new one renders anything a fan wrote. Walk Apple's list (terms, filter, flag, block that notifies, action within 24 hours) on the destination, signed out, before merging. And **a column needs the filter from the day a public page renders it**, not from when it was written: before shipping a page that shows a table's text, check that the table has a `filter_objectionable_text` trigger. The fix is `SafetyMenu` in [`CommunityIssueArticle`](src/components/CommunityIssueArticle.tsx) and `20261007235500_filter_slot_notes.sql`, and the comment above the shelf link in [`Songbook.tsx`](src/pages/Songbook.tsx) now says the controls have to travel with the link.

- **A source-grep guard on a null-versus-zero rule must anchor on the end of the statement, and the rule itself is pinned only by rendering the page.** #98 made the admin traffic tiles show "—" rather than 0 when PostHog does not answer. Gate 8 (2026-10-08, on `ef74223`) got two kinds of edit past its tests. `const traffic = toAdminTraffic(trafficRows) ?? { source: "posthog", ...zeros }` satisfied the regex `toAdminTraffic\(trafficRows\)`, which had no `;`, and the page could not catch it either, because a zero payload carrying the right tag is valid. And because no test rendered `Admin.tsx`, 8 of 9 mutants on the page survived: 0 for an unanswered query, a tile printing its neighbour's figure, a caption claiming PostHog when PostHog had not answered. The regex now ends in `;`, and [`adminTrafficPanel.test.tsx`](src/pages/__tests__/adminTrafficPanel.test.tsx) renders the page and asserts the "—", the "0" and the caption as they appear on screen, with five different figures so that a swapped tile fails. Same family as the `Messages.tsx` correction above: a page no test imports has no regression test, whatever the greps around it say.

- **An auth gate is verified only when every real caller has been run through it after the deploy. A probe you wrote is not a caller.** #113 added a who-may-send check to `send-transactional-email` and verified it with five production probes: the anon key, a forged service JWT, the database's vault key, an admin and a fan. None of those is how notify-dm, notify-comment and the daily and weekly reports call it. They use `supabase.functions.invoke` on a service-role client, and that bearer does not arrive as sent: it is neither the function's own key nor a token Supabase confirms as the service role. So every one of those sends was refused with 401. The refusal logged nothing, and it surfaced only when a test DM on 2026-10-08 produced no email. The callers now prove the key with [`_shared/internalSend.ts`](supabase/functions/_shared/internalSend.ts), and refusals log what kind of bearer arrived. [`internalSend.test.ts`](src/lib/__tests__/internalSend.test.ts) finds every `functions.invoke('send-transactional-email'` under `supabase/functions/` and fails on any call without the header. Before shipping a gate, grep for every caller (`functions.invoke('<name>'`, `functions/v1/<name>`, and the database's `net.http_post`). After the deploy, drive each caller end to end in production.
